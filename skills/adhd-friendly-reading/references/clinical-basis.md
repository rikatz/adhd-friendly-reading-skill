# Clinical basis of AFRS (short form)

What clinical research on ADHD says, and what each finding means for how a
response should be written. Evidence strength is stated honestly. The evidence column rates the finding,
not the AFRS rule: no rule has been tested as an intervention with ADHD readers.
AFRS is a readability aid, not a treatment, and makes no clinical claims.
Full citations: [sources.md](sources.md).

| Model or finding | Key source | Evidence | What it means for writing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ADHD is a valid, heterogeneous condition; many findings are supported by meta-analyses | World Federation of ADHD International Consensus Statement, 208 conclusions (Faraone et al., 2021) | Strong (consensus over large studies and meta-analyses) | No one-size-fits-all: every AFRS feature can be switched off |
| Adult ADHD is often lifelong; treatment combines psychoeducation, CBT and medication, tailored to the person | Updated European Consensus Statement on adult ADHD (Kooij et al., 2019) | Expert consensus (63 experts) | Write for capable adults; never infantilize |
| ADHD as a disorder of executive function and self-regulation; problems are of *performance* (using what you know) more than of knowledge | Barkley, 1997 (*Psychological Bulletin*) and later work | Influential theory, broad support for EF deficits | Externalize information at the point of use; restate instead of "see above" |
| Dual pathway: executive dysfunction *and* delay aversion (motivational) as separate routes | Sonuga-Barke, 2003 | Moderate; replicated dissociation, effects vary by person | Payoff first; each section opens with its takeaway; no long build-ups |
| Default-mode interference: the "mind-wandering" brain network fails to switch off during tasks | Sonuga-Barke & Castellanos, 2007; mind-wandering account (Bozhilova et al., 2018); mega-analysis of large samples (Norman et al., 2023) | Partial support, small effects | Expect drift; make text resumable with landmarks and progress markers |
| Timing and time-perception deficits | Meta-analyses: Zheng et al., 2022; Marx et al., 2022 | Moderate (meta-analytic) | Explicit sequence; grounded time estimates in plans; mark urgency |
| If-then plans (implementation intentions) improve inhibition, shifting and delay of gratification in children with ADHD | Gawrilow & Gollwitzer, 2008; Gawrilow, Gollwitzer & Oettingen, 2011 | Moderate (experimental, children who formed their own plans) | By analogy: next steps as concrete "If X, then Y" actions |
| CBT for adults: organizing and planning, coping with distractibility, adaptive thinking | Safren et al., 2010, *JAMA* randomized controlled trial; therapy manual *Mastering Your Adult ADHD* | One RCT (n = 86); the link to a text outline is an analogy | By analogy: broken-down steps and a visible outline |
| Emotional dysregulation is a frequent, impairing feature of adult ADHD | Shaw et al., 2014; Faraone et al., 2019 practitioner review; meta-analysis by Beheshti et al., 2020 | Moderate to strong | Neutral, non-judgmental tone; no shaming language |
| Working-memory training improves trained tasks but transfers little to symptoms or daily function | Cortese et al., 2015 (European ADHD Guidelines Group); Melby-Lervåg & Hulme, 2013 | Strong (meta-analyses) | Reduce the load in the text instead of expecting the reader to hold more |
| "Interest-based nervous system" (interest, challenge, novelty, urgency) | Dodson (clinical observation) | Weak: not tested in controlled studies | Only the safe part is used: tie content to the reader's goal. No gimmicks |

## Not used, and why

- **Bionic Reading:** controlled tests found no effect on reading speed.
- **Color gradients (BeeLine):** mixed independent evidence; cannot be rendered in AI chat interfaces.
- **Simplified "Easy Read" language:** designed for intellectual disability; would infantilize the target readers.
